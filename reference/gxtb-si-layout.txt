In the following, we provide a detailed derivation of the general eXtended tight-binding (g-
xTB) method, including all relevant energy contributions. This includes the derivation of the
underlying tight-binding (TB) energy expression from Kohn-Sham (KS) density functional
theory (DFT) (Sec. 1.1), as well as a detailed description of the applied approximations to
arrive at all components of the total energy (Secs. 1.6 and 1.12).

1.1.2   Step 2: Shift of the reference state

KS-DFT describes the energy change upon formation of an atom, molecule, or solid starting
from the reference state of infinitely separated electrons and bare nuclei (even in the FC ap-
proximation, where the contribution of the core electrons is merely constant). Consequently,
the numerical value of the KS-DFT energy is generally several orders of magnitude larger
than chemically relevant relative energies and arises from the cancellation of even larger
contributions of opposite sign. The fundamental idea of DFTB is to shift the reference state
to resemble the system of interest more closely. As a result, the magnitude of the total
DFTB energy is substantially smaller than the total DFT energy and lends itself well to
approximations. A chemically reasonable choice for the reference state is the ensemble of
neutral atoms constituting the molecule or solid, augmented with a (positive or negative)
number of infinitely separated electrons to restore the correct overall charge. The neutral
atoms remain in a spherical unperturbed reference state (not necessarily the atomic ground
state, see below for details) and are placed in their final molecular positions to construct the
reference density ρ0 . The overall molecular density can be written as


                                         ρ = ρ0 + δρ                                        (4)


with the density fluctuations δρ. The density fluctuations describe the density difference
between the ensemble of neutral atoms and the true density of the system. It is important
to note that these fluctuations need not be particle number-conserving. Instead, δρ contains



                                               5
also the density change necessary to reproduce the total charge of a system, i.e.,

                                       Z
                                           δρ(~r) d~r = qtot .                               (5)


   The change in reference state for DFTB is based on the pioneering work on a reference
density-based density functional approximation by Harris 4 and the partitioning of the KS-
DFT energy by Foulkes and Haydock. 5 The latter proceeds in three key steps: (i) the true
density is split according to Eq. 4, (ii) Hartree- and XC potentials of the reference density are
added to the Hamiltonian, and (iii) double-counted terms of the Hartree and XC potentials
are subtracted. The resulting expression contains the Hamiltonian of the reference density
ρ0 applied to the KS-molecular orbitals (MOs) representing the total density, the repulsive
nuclear repulsion with a canceling attractive (inverse sign) Coulomb interaction between the
reference density, a repulsive (inverse sign) XC potential applied to the reference density, as
well as further second- and higher-order terms. 5,6 DFTB collects the correction terms in the
zeroth-order interatomic repulsion, leaving only second-order terms aside from the Hamil-
tonian of the reference density. While this partitioning is exact, the accuracy of the final
SQM method depends on the quality of the approximation made for each respective term.
Particularly crucial are the reference density-based zeroth-order repulsion and the combined
zeroth- and first-order Hamiltonian, which are frequently approximated via pairwise numer-
ical functions (so-called Slater-Koster tables). 6–8 To enable element-wise parametrization,
eXtended tight-binding (xTB) relies instead on an effective extended Hückel-type Hamilto-
nian constructed from modified atomic energy levels. While ”classical” contributions such
as the kinetic energy or Hartree potential of the reference density can be naturally expressed
as a sum over atomic contributions, the non-linear XC functional of the (overlapping) ref-
erence density may differ significantly from the sum of atomic contributions. Consequently,
we choose to partition the KS-DFT energy in Eq. 3b differently for g-xTB, such that the
effective Hamiltonian naturally decomposes into atomic contribution.

its original KS-DFT reference state, since we replace the full non-local correlation energy

employs the Mulliken charges as part of a non-linear scaling function for the total dynamic
polarizabilities of interacting atoms. Consequently, the DFT-D4 energy with a given set of
Mulliken charges provides the full dispersion energy of the system, instead of describing the
differential change in dispersion relative to a reference state.

Multipole approximations have been used before in the context of DFTB 44–48 and are also
part of the GFN2-xTB method. 13 However, GFN2-xTB performs the multipole expansion
only up to the second order, including monopole-monopole, monopole-dipole, dipole-dipole,
and monopole-quadrupole terms. This approach, while consistent in the order of the mul-
tipole expansion, lacks consistent interactions between all the final multipole moments (i.e.
missing dipole-quadrupole and quadrupole-quadrupole terms). Following recent develop-
ments of mDFTB by Vuong et al., 48 we employ in g-xTB up to fourth-order terms in the
multipole expansion and include all interactions between monopole, dipole, and quadrupole
moments. In contrast to prior Methods such as GFN2-xTB or mDFTB, we consider only
the pure Coulomb repulsion operator without short-range XC effects (covered by ACPs in
Sec. 1.8), avoiding any SCF instabilities due to short-range multipole terms.

1.16     Onsite correction for Mulliken-approximated Fock exchange

The onsite correction for the Mulliken-approximated Fock exchange corrects for missing
terms in the Mulliken-based approximation of the range-separated exact exchange in Eq. 145
admixed in the g-xTB energy expression (see Eq. 15). The Mulliken approximation for four
center integrals (µλ|κν) (chemists’ notation) vanishes if either hφµ |φλ i = 0 or hφκ |φν i = 0,
which is the case for orbitals of different angular momentum at one center. With the onsite
correction developed by Dominguez et al., 63–66 one can reintroduce the missing integrals and
correct this effective zero differential overlap (ZDO) approximation for orbitals at the same
atom.


1.16.1    Energy

We approximate the range-separated Fock exchange expressed in the AO basis in Eq. 146
again, but now based on the more elaborate Rüdenberg approximation for four-center inte-
grals at one center. 26,64,67 For an in-depth derivation, we recommend Ref. 64. During the
derivation, it is crucial to make sure that the rotational invariance of the total energy is not
broken. In the HF-based intermediate neglect of differential overlap (INDO) method, this re-
quires only the relaxation of the ZDO approximation 68 for orbitals at the same atom. INDO
retains a rotationally invariant total energy since both Coulomb- and exchange integrals
are approximated consistently. 26,39,69 In g-xTB, we approximate the Coulomb interaction
partitioned into contributions from the reference density and density fluctuations while the
exchange interaction works with the full valence density. Hence, a direct inclusion of only
Fock exchange integrals at one atom would break the rotational invariance of the g-xTB
total energy. 39

2      Additional Details on First-Principles Constants

2.1        Spin-polarization constants

The ten element-wise reference spin constants Wll00 (for s-s0, s-p, s-d, s-f , p-p0, p-d, p-f , d-d0,
d-f , and f -f 0) were computed with the ωB97M-V functional and the def2-QZVPP 85 (def-
TZVP for ANs) basis set following the procedure laid out in Ref. 54. All atomic calculations
employ the ridft module of Turbomole exploiting full point-group symmetry. The elec-
tronic configurations follow the Aufbau principle without the exceptions in the ground state
occupations that lead to discontinuous filling of the shells (i.e., Cr). For the TMs, we fill the
d orbitals consistently either based on a filled s shell in the fourth and sixth row or based
on a half-filled s shell in the fifth row. For the LNs, the 4f orbitals are populated before the
5d orbitals starting from Ce, while the ANs leave one 6d electron present while filling the 5f
shell. Within each shell we assign the same fractional occupation to every orbital, ensuring
that the numbers of α and β electrons are equal and no net spin density arises from uneven
spin populations.


2.2        One-center exchange integrals

Analytical one-center exchange integrals used in Sec. 1.16 were computed at the HF level
using the full q-vSZP basis set, without approximations such as chain-of-spheres algorithm
for the exchange term (COSX) or resolution of the identity (RI). All calculations were per-
formed with ORCA 6.0.1. 86 To extract the exchange integrals of the form (µ ν | µ ν) from
the ORCA output, a publicly available Python script was used (github.com/marcelmbn/1cX-
CintegralsfromORCA), which leverages the ORCA_2JSON utility for convenient data access.
In g-xTB, nine shell-resolved interactions (s-p, p-p0 , s-d, p-d, d-d0 , s-f , p-f , d-f , f -f 0 ) are
considered, requiring the explicit integrals over AO to be averaged. For example, the p–p0
interaction is obtained by averaging over the three distinct components: px –py , px –pz , and
py –pz .

3.2.1     Total Energies

In most applications involving relative energetics, error compensation due to chemical sim-
ilarity significantly reduces the inaccuracies of electronic structure methods. As a result,
relative conformer energies can typically be predicted with much higher accuracy than reac-
tion energies involving chemically dissimilar species.
   A stringent, unbiased assessment of a method’s energetic accuracy – free from the benefit
of error cancellation as in usual applications – relies on the comparison of absolute total
electronic energies. To this end, we extracted a subset from the QCML dataset. 87 While
the full dataset comprises over 33.5 million DFT data points, we randomly selected 100,000
entries and applied the following filtering steps:

   • Charge restrictions: Molecules with formal charges below −1 or above +2 were
        excluded. Strongly negative charges on small saturated species can lead to Rydberg-
        like states requiring diffuse functions, which are unstable and chemically unrealistic in
        most practical contexts. Similarly, highly positive charges on small systems are rarely
        encountered in realistic settings.

   • Elemental constraints: Data points containing lanthanides or transition metals were
        excluded due to unreliable spin states and chemically questionable structures.

   • Removal of redundant structures: Molecules with identical molecular connectivity
        graphs (i.e., stereoisomers) were filtered out to avoid bias toward certain scaffolds
        represented multiple times in different configurations.

   • Outlier removal: Structures flagged as outliers by the dataset authors were excluded
        from further analysis.

   Following these filtering steps, the dataset was reduced from 100,000 structures to 77,477
unique molecules. While the breadth of the original dataset is impressive, certain aspects

                                               101
reduce its utility for benchmarking electronic structure methods. Inclusion of Rydberg-
like states with unbound electrons and negative electron affinities deviates significantly from
chemically realistic systems. Redundant data points differing only in questionable spin states,
as well as mislabeled or physically implausible structures – e.g., H–H distances of 0.5 Å or
molecules like F–Gd–H treated as closed-shell species – further limit the applicability of
such entries in method development. We suggest a more thorough curation and physical
sanity check for future dataset releases. For each remaining entry, we computed single-point
energies, nuclear gradients, and dipole moments using ωB97M-V/aTZ, r2 SCAN-3c, and g-
xTB.

   Fig. 2 displays a histogram of total electronic energy deviations between g-xTB and
ωB97M-V/aTZ. The standard deviation for the total energy amounts to 0.0070 Eh (4.4 kcal mol−1 ),
with a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.0075 Eh and a root-mean-square error (RMSE) of
0.0103 Eh . These small deviations are sufficient to serve as a sensitive test for problematic
SCF solutions of the reference method for complicated systems. However, in contrast to
machine learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs), the accuracy for total energies does not

determine the final accuracy for relative energies. Relative g-xTB energies generally ex-
hibit far smaller errors on similar systems due to robust error cancellation enabled by its
physics-based construction.
